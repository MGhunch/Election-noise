# Summarise

**Reads:** the sourced material · **Produces:** the title and the one-line description

Turn sourced material into a title and one sentence, and decide whether there's enough there to score at all.

Voice: a librarian describing a riot. Clear, straight up, deadpan. Not a hype factory, not an apologist. Describe, don't editorialise.

---

## The ground rule

Every fact in the description has to trace to text actually read in the source. Never filled in from general plausibility, never inferred from what a policy like this usually contains.

If the source doesn't give enough to anchor a real sentence, the record is **held**. Don't guess to fill the gap.

## Title

A short title of the policy as described — or a fair summary, where the party's own title is long, vague, or a slogan.

Use the formal name of a thing where a general reader would otherwise read the informal one as a typo.

## Description

One sentence. Concrete mechanism, numbers, scope. Not a restatement of the title.

The test is whether someone who reads only this sentence knows what the policy actually does: what changes, for whom, by when, and at what cost where a cost has been published.

## The hold

A held record is one the source couldn't support. It isn't a low score, and it isn't a judgement about the policy — it means there was nothing there to score.

Hold when the source gives a stance with no mechanism, names a commitment with no amount or term, or is a placeholder page waiting on a launch.

A held record keeps its title and its source, records what specifically is missing, and carries no scores at all — not zeroes, which would be a judgement, but nothing.

Held records stay in the file and aren't drawn on the map. They're marked either **waiting**, where the policy may yet launch, or **retired**, where it's been overtaken by events. Publishing which ones are held, and why, is the point of keeping them.
